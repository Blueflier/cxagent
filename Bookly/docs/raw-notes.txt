Steps i did:

Brainstorm primitives of customer support agents:

Primitive
Purpose
Example
Answer
Provide information or explain a policy.
“Standard delivery takes 3–5 business days.”
Clarify
Get missing information needed to help.
“Did the wrong title arrive, or the wrong edition?”
Acknowledge
Show understanding of the issue or emotion.
“That’s frustrating, especially since it was a gift.”
Confirm
Check understanding or get authorization before an action.
“Should I send the replacement to your original address?”
Act
Use a tool to inspect or change something.
Look up tracking, cancel an order, issue a refund.
Escalate
Offer or initiate a human handoff.
“I’ll pass this to our support team with the details we’ve collected.”
Decline
Explain a limit and, when possible, offer an alternative.
“I can’t change an order after dispatch, but I can help arrange a return.”
Redirect
Bring an off-topic conversation back into scope.
“I can help with book recommendations or your order. Which would you like?”
Close
End after resolution, disengagement, or a necessary boundary.
“Your replacement is confirmed. You’re all set.”

One thing i dont understand tho: in my opinion, people only buy if they Save money, Make money, Or reduce risk. So im going to research
Ok saving money makes sense, often times you have to hire many people to answer, and it has to be global or you would just have hours 8-5pm. And thats only assuming these people are calling 8 hours a day - sometimes they are just sitting around which means wasted money for you
Furthermore, you also need to onboard these employees which takes even more money as your business scales. And not every employee performs the same, sometimes people have bad days
Making money: upselling, when someone returns something - figuring out why is important and also showing empathy to not lose a customer. This is one is a bit trickier because honestly people are more empathetic than a chatbot - however, if the answer is inaccurate and/or slow - you also risk having a bad customer interaction
Upselling is also a possibility, when someone inquires about a book for example, the agent can also pull up statistics like similar books that other people have bought / enjoys. Obviously people could just ask an LLM but imagine if this was a clothing store, then similar clothes that people buy together can be recommended, or if this was a restaurant - understanding supply and demand economics of what to order weekly to see what people enjoy eating
